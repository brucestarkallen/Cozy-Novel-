# Cozy-Novel-

> # ⚠️ TO ANYONE FIXING THIS APP: MINUTES, NOT HOURS ⚠️
> ## Find the root cause by reading the code. Run ONLY the tests your change reaches — never the whole repo for a small fix, never the same suite twice. Ship, then say what you didn't run. The owner is waiting. Full rule at the top of [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md).

A quiet little room for writing a novel with a model beside you — on your phone, in Termux, in any browser.

Choose your writing world: **Academy of Ink**, a magical library with arched windows and drifting ink; **Neon Tokyo**, a rain-wet street of glowing signs, its last train and rain moving through it, the page and its buttons lit like the signs at night and plain by day; or **Original Study**, the familiar picture of a lamplit room and rainy window. Academy and Tokyo are drawn from shapes and lines. Original Study frames its portrait picture on narrow screens and its wider room on unfolded phones and desktops. All three have light and dark interfaces. Switch worlds from the shelf, or **More → Your writing room** while writing. Ambient animation can be paused and respects reduced-motion preferences; the room can also be hidden for an uninterrupted page.

You write the book chapter by chapter. Say what happens next and press **Write**, and your writer writes it onto the page; or write the words yourself and press **Add as my words**. Nothing is ever thrown away: another take or a rewrite is a new *version* of the passage, the earlier one an arrow away. A change made to the words themselves — your **Edit**, a fix from the checker, your partner's words — goes on the page itself, the way a correction does, and **⋯ → Put back the words before** brings back what it replaced, one change at a time — the last four changes, and always the words as they were first written.

While you write, a quiet **helper** keeps the book's notes — the people (who they are, how they stand with each other, where they are now), the places, the things worth remembering, the threads still open — and a summary of every chapter, so a book far longer than your model can read still fits. A **partner** talks the book over with you like a seasoned novelist, and when you ask it makes the change itself, each with its Undo. In **Scenes** you step into the story as one of its people while the narrator plays everyone else, then turn what happened into prose.

Your books live on the phone, in `~/.cozynovel`, never in a browser: close every tab, clear the browser, open another one — the book is exactly where you left it, and every open window shows the same book without a refresh.

## Install (once)

In Termux:

```sh
pkg install git python
git clone https://github.com/brucestarkallen/Cozy-Novel-.git ~/cozynovel
cd ~/cozynovel && bash install.sh
```

From then on, one word does everything:

```sh
cozynovel
```

It updates itself, starts the server (or replaces an older one that is still running), keeps the phone awake while you write, and opens **http://127.0.0.1:8081**. The server goes on running in the background after you leave Termux.

### Coming from the first Cozy Novel

Run this once:

```sh
cd ~/cozynovel && git pull && bash install.sh
```

then `cozynovel`. The first time it opens, your books, their chapters, notes, drafts and conversations, and your connections come across by themselves, once. The first Cozy Novel's own files are only read, never changed. (If you only pull and then type `cozynovel`, that works too: the new server takes the port from the old one and turns the command into the new one for next time.)

### On a computer

```sh
git clone https://github.com/brucestarkallen/Cozy-Novel-.git ~/cozynovel
cd ~/cozynovel
python3 serve.py
```

and open **http://127.0.0.1:8081**. Another port: `COZYNOVEL_PORT=8091 python3 serve.py`. Another folder for the books: `COZYNOVEL_HOME=/path python3 serve.py`.

## Writing

- **The shelf** is your books, the one you touched last first. **A new book** asks only for a working title; what it's about and how it reads can come later (or paste a whole plot essential).
- **The page** is the chapter. Tap a passage for its controls: **Another take**, **Rewrite…** (with a note on what should change — the change becomes what happens, and the writer follows it through everything it touches, never just patching the old words), **Edit**, and **⋯** for the rest — what the writer was sent, copy, *write from here* (a new passage after this one), a scene break, *start a new chapter from here*, remove. Under the controls a line says the passage's number, its words, how many tokens its request was (*about* when the provider gave no count of its own) and when. While a page is written you can scroll up and read: the page stays where you put it, and **Follow the writing** — beside **Suggestions** at the page's foot, side by side — takes you back down to the words as they come. A line at the bottom (*Put in on the page*, with **Undo**) shows over the part of the screen you are working in — the page, a pane beside or over it, a scene, a sheet — just above its box and the row at its foot, never over a button. **Premise** (beside Edit) shows what you typed in the box for that page, whole, and puts it away again — hidden until you press it, so the page reads as a book (the box empties once the page is written). Its own **Edit**, in that line, opens the premise in a box to change; **Resend**, there only while the box is open, writes the page again from the words in it, as a new version — the one you had stays an arrow back. What you type there stays until the page comes or you press **Cancel**, even with Premise put away. Another take writes the page again from the premise of the version on show, and a rewrite keeps it; a page you let the writer choose (Continue), your own words and a scene's prose have none, and no Premise button. A page that did not come says why at the end of the chapter: **Retry** asks for it again from the same premise, in the same place — a *write from here* stays where you asked for it — and **Edit premise** puts that premise back in its box, asking first if the box has other words in it.
- **The scroller** is a slim bar in the page's right gutter — it never covers a word; drag it to move through a long chapter quickly. Which passage is under your eye (*Passage 12 of 40*) shows in the bar above the page, beside the chapter's name, and beside the scroller while you hold it.
- **Select a few words** and the one button says **Rewrite**: only those words are written again, the rest of the passage stays exactly as it is.
- **Stop** keeps what came. A passage that stopped partway says so and offers **Go on**, which carries it on from its last word.
- **A new page is made whole** before it is kept: a heading or a plan the writer put above the story, and a note to you, its check of its own work or a block of tracking after it, come off and are kept with the passage — **⋯ → Put back what was taken off** brings them back. A story's own first line, a narrator's own question and speech are never taken. Web page or code markup left loose in the story — a tag standing alone like a stray `</code>`, a line of ``` — comes off too, and is said under the page; italics and bold written as tags become the book's own, and HTML the page truly carries (a `<div>` around its words) stays. Pages kept from before lose it once when the book opens; in your own words, the checker offers the sentence without it.
- **Speech marks pair up.** Each paragraph's quotation marks are read in order, the way a reader pairs them. A mark standing where the missing quote belongs — an asterisk, or a single quote after a comma, never one beside another quote — becomes that quote; a pair written in two kinds ("…” or “…") is written in one kind; speech wrapped in asterisks loses the asterisks — only speech wrapped alone (`*"Ahh—!"*`), never the sounds either side of a spoken line (`*CLANG!* "Nngh—!" *THUD*`) and never bold; an asterisk left open closes where its stretch ends, never after the narration. A letter, a song or a speech that runs over several paragraphs and closes in a later one is read as one; a quotation ending in a figure (`"Top 10"`, `"Catch-22"`) is a quotation, a measure (`a 12" blade`, `5'9"`) a measure. Marks only, never a word — on a new page, a scene's telling and your partner's words, and once on the pages kept before (2.12.35 parted again the sounds 2.12.34 had glued to the speech between them, `*CLANG!"Nngh—!"THUD*`). A new page or telling that came as one block of more than 900 characters is parted where speech begins, never inside a speech (white space only). A quote simply left out (`"Tck. Tck. "You did not look…`) leaves no mark to turn: it is found, and the checker sets it (below).
- **A space a slip left out or put in** is set right — after a full stop, a question or exclamation mark between two sentences (`ash.She`), after a comma (`yes,she`, `"Fine",she`), around speech marks (`"Go,"she said`, `he said,"Go."`), and one standing before a comma (`word , next`) — white space only, never in a figure (3,000, 9:30), a measure (`an 8"x10" photo`), an initialism (U.S.A., Ph.D.), a title like Re:Zero, an address, a tag, `code` or an entity (`&mdash;`), or a page in another language's marks; never the last paragraph of a page cut short. On a new page as it arrives, a scene's telling and your partner's words; once on the pages kept before; and in your own words the checker offers it, no model asked.
- **The box at the bottom**: what happens next — your premise, written masterfully — or leave it empty and press **Continue**: the writer chooses what happens next, whatever this story most needs now (true to its people, to what the chapter is for and to the threads still open), and writes it onto the page in front of you, one passage at a time — how much to write (Automatic — the writer judges how much the story needs and stops where your next premise comes in — or a few paragraphs, a scene, a long stretch, the rest of the chapter), **who decides how it turns out**, and who writes. **Writer decides**: your premise sets it going and the writer carries it through to where the story's own logic lands it — who comes out ahead, what it costs. **I decide**: the writer plays everything up to it in full and stops on the moment it hangs, for your next premise to settle. Either way the writer writes everyone — the main character too — and how everyone takes it; whatever your premise already says happens, happens. What your premise explains — how a power works and its limits, how many, why, what someone plans — is for the writer only: in the story people know only what they have lived, seen, heard or been told (and what anyone there would plainly make of it), unless your premise says they learn more; the same goes for a rewrite's note, Go on, and the notes themselves. Each book keeps its own.
- **The book's title** at the top opens the chapters; **Notes**, **Partner**, **Search** and **⋯** (Scenes, About this book, Full screen, Reading mode, Settings) sit beside it. On a wide screen the chapters and notes stand beside the page.
- **Search** (the magnifier) finds any words — in **This book** or **All books**: the passages as they stand on the page, chapter names, the notes, what the book is about, the partner and the scenes. Capitals, accents, curly quotes, line breaks, italics and the way a dash is typed don't matter. Tap a place and you're there, the words marked for a moment.
- **Full screen** is the writing page with nothing above it — the bars go (and the browser's own, where it allows), the page and the box stay; moving to the next chapter keeps it. **Reading mode** is only the page, for reading. A scene has its own full screen button, and so does the partner — each in a head that stays at the top of its pane while the turns scroll under it, so Full screen and Leave full screen are always in reach. Back, or the small button in the corner, brings the bars back. The notes' light, which rides on the Notes button in the bar, stands beside **Leave full screen** in every full screen — the page's, a scene's and the partner's — in the same colours (amber while the helper reads, green when the notes are up to date, red when something is wrong); a tap says how the notes stand.
- **About this book**: *What it's about* and *How it reads* are each **Manual** (only your words) or **Automatic** (your words, if any, and what the helper keeps of the book as it stands — current as the book goes on). Correct what the helper keeps and your correction stands until you press *Write it from the book again*. **Sound** is **Comic** (the world's sounds as sound effects in asterisks — \*CLANG!\* — as in Cozy Tavern) or **Novel** (the same sounds written into the sentences); spoken words stay in quotes; nonverbal sounds follow the chosen voice, without a forced quota. Words brought from SillyTavern that say {{user}} and {{char}} are read by every model as names: {{user}} as your name, {{char}} as the name you give it here (asked only when your words have the marks).
- **The story map** — for a book you mean to finish. In a chapter's **⋯** in the chapter list, **What this chapter is for** keeps your plan for it (it shows under the chapter's name); the writer reads it only while writing in that chapter, as where it is going, never as something that has happened. **Make it the last chapter** tells the writer, in that chapter only, how you mean the book to end (About this book → *How it ends*) and which threads in the notes are still open, so it brings the book home there. *How long you mean it to be, in words* in About this book shows how far the book has come in the chapter list, and a note says each quarter of the way once. Your partner sees the whole map and can write it with you: **Quick asks → Map the chapters to the end** plans the chapters still to come, a new empty chapter made for each plan past the last, every one with Undo.
- **A chapter as long as you like.** A chapter longer than a third of your writer's room is kept in stretches of about 2,500 words, and the helper sums each one up as it finishes. When the chapter no longer fits beside everything else, the writer gets its earliest stretches by their summaries and the newest words whole — never a middle cut out — and the chapters before keep their summaries beside it.
- The phone's **Back** closes whatever is open — a menu, a sheet, a drawer, the edit box (keeping what you typed) — before it ever leaves the book.

## The notes, the summaries and the partner

- **Notes → Timeline** follows meaningful changes in relationships, people and threads, in manuscript order. Filter by person or thread, choose **Up to here** or **Whole book**, and tap a source to return to its exact passage. Quotes support each turn; beliefs, claims and intentions say whose they are. The existing cards remain your current notes and the place to edit them. Choosing another passage version changes its history too. Scenes enter this history when turned into manuscript prose. The helper gathers it during its usual reading; opening the Timeline makes no model call. **It heals itself**: a page read before the Timeline was kept, or whose reading gave no turns for it, is read for its turns alone by the helper, a few at a time, once the notes have nothing else to read — the Timeline says how many are left and never asks you to read the whole book again; words changed on the page itself (a fix, the spaces, a name changed everywhere, words put back) keep their turns, as they keep the bibliography's facts, turn by turn wherever a turn's quotes still stand — quotes read as a reader reads them (curly or straight marks, a dash, "..." for "…", runs of spaces) and kept as the page has them. A turn or a fact a change cannot carry — a name changed everywhere before a **Go on** was read, a fix, your **Rewrite** (of the passage or of words you selected) or your partner's words for the passage that reword a quoted phrase — is never dropped quietly: the passage is read whole again by itself, one ask for the Timeline and one for the bibliography (a long one in parts) — so nearly every whole rewrite, yours or the checker's when its fixes go in by themselves, costs one Timeline ask and one bibliography ask more — and what the change's own reading found is kept; facts the bibliography was still reading in parts are read on to the end. A reading still on its way when a name is changed everywhere — changed, changed again, or changed and put back with Undo, in this browser or another whatever its clock says — keeps all it found: what it found of the words the name stands in is carried through the change with the rest of the page, and what it found of the other pages it read says the name as it is now; nothing is read again for that. Everything else on its way then — a chapter's summary or a stretch's, What it's about, the notes' check, the checker's fix, a scene's telling — takes the new name when it lands too, but for words it quotes from a page the change never touched. A name changed back by hand is a change like any other: only Undo puts the words back as they were. A page you edit, move or pick another take of while it is out is read again whole, as any page you change. A name changed everywhere reaches what was kept of the pages it never stood in as well — their turns, the facts that name it, a fix the checker offers, carried where it stands (one it cannot carry is let go and the page checked again — never a fix kept that cannot apply) — and its Undo puts them back. The words before keep theirs, so Undo and **Put back the words before** bring them back with no reading, and the same turn read again shows once, while two turns that say different things show as two, however near their words. Two turns telling the same thing between the same people, read at different times, show as one — the newer reading's — whichever side tells it ("Emilia meets Jovan at the gate." and "Jovan meets Emilia at the gate."); opposite deeds told in one reading ("Emilia betrays Jovan." and "Jovan betrays Emilia." of one sentence) stay two. A long page is read in parts. A page — for the notes, the Timeline or the bibliography — its model is only slow on is asked again with a longer wait each time; a try is counted only once even the longest wait (a quarter of an hour) passes — its second try is asked by itself once as long again has passed, never at once — nothing else is asked of that model until then — and the Timeline says when ("1 passage is not read for the Timeline yet — the helper's model was too slow to answer for it, so the helper reads it again by itself in fifteen minutes, and its turns show here as they are read."); pages its model will not read together are read a page at a time from the next run, by themselves. A page its model will not give its turns for after two tries is passed by and said so, with what it found; a page set aside is said apart, with **Read it again** under Cards; a helper whose connection was turned away is said stopped, with **Try again now**. From a person's or a thread's card, **On the Timeline** shows its turns through the whole book.
- **People** (who they are, *with others* — how they stand with the people who matter to them, and why — and *right now*), **Places**, **Things worth remembering** (objects, facts, secrets and who knows them, rules of the world) and **Threads** (promises, secrets, questions still open) are what the writer reads before every page, so a name, a scar or a promise never drifts. **Chapter summaries** are something else: they stand in for whole chapters once the book is longer than the model can read (the newest chapters always ride whole; a chapter is never sent both whole and in short). A chapter's summary runs to about a twelfth of it, up to 1,200 words. A long chapter — past a third of the writer's room, or past 14,400 words on any model — is also kept in stretches of about 2,500 words, each summed up as the writing passes it; its one summary is made from them — and a long chapter that cannot ride whole rides by those stretches' summaries while the room holds them, before its one summary — the newest of them partly whole when room is left: its earliest stretches in short, the rest word for word; a summary that loses a name or a figure, or gives one the chapter never does, is asked once more; a summary from before a change never rides — a long chapter goes by its unchanged stretches meanwhile, a short one by its opening and close. **The summary check** holds every summary the helper writes — a chapter's, a stretch's, a scene's — once against its own words (Cozy Tavern's continuous audit, for a book) and puts right what it gets wrong, taking a mend only when the wrong words are in the summary and the right ones in the chapter, and an addition only when the chapter says it; summaries written before it are checked too, a few at a time. See each one, its stretches and what the check made of it in the chapter's ⋯ → **Its summary**. A **long scene** is kept in stretches the same way: once it grows long for the narrator's room, its earliest turns ride as their summaries instead of being left out (⋯ → **The scene so far, in short**).
- **Quick add** (at the top of Notes → **Bibliography**): tell it what you want in your world in your own words — a council and who sits on it, a power and what it costs, a rule, a person — anything not in your premise or the book yet. Your partner's connection holds it against the book first (the passages that name what you add, whole), your premises, your bibliography and the notes, and answers: what goes into the bibliography — in at once as your entries, your partner asked to write each up the way a series bible is written: your meaning, in clear, finished sentences, the grammar, spelling and wording put right and a name the book already has spelled its way, never your words pasted as you typed them; an entry already there asked only for what your words add to it, and put together with what your partner wrote — each sentence of yours kept, or the new sentence in its place where it keeps every word of yours that tells, says "not" to each thing as you do, and brings nothing your words do not say (your grammar and spelling put right); the words your newer word changes put right where they stand — in your partner's words for them, put in by their shape (a few words in place of yours where they read there, their stop let go where your sentence goes on; a whole sentence in place of the sentence of yours that holds those words only where it keeps every other word of it that tells, its names, its figures and its "not"), or its sentence that carries it — never left beside it, never pasted in or said twice; where none of its words go in, asked once more by itself and still none, nothing goes in for that newer word: your entry, your line and What it's about stand as you wrote them (your words are never spliced in), and the row says it *did not go in — your partner gave no words for it* where it gave none, or *did not go in — its words for it could not go in as they were*, with **Ask again**; only what brings what you say now added (a word your entry says only under another's "not" is new: "Emilia drinks blood" beside your "Her brother Dane does not drink blood"), never a sentence of yours said again in other words, nor one saying "not" otherwise than you do, nor one that would say both a sentence of yours and another way of it — held only where the words leave no doubt: the same one, the same thing told the same way (where one lives, an age, a count, a colour) with another value and no change listed, whoever counted it ("lives in Prague" beside your "lives in Venice", "three brothers" beside your "two brothers", "green eyes" beside your "grey eyes", "a red cloak" beside your "black cloak"), or your "not" said otherwise of the same one told the same way — asked for once more by itself, then held under *Not put in*: *it would say both — your partner listed no change for your words*, with **Ask again**; what its words leave in doubt goes in, said on the row with **Undo these** ("is human" beside your "is a vampire", her raven's green eyes beside your grey ones, the blood "of animals" beside your "not human blood", one more of a thing said so — "a white raven too"); where your newer words say "not" to words of yours ("not her brother", "no longer lives in Venice", "instead of her steward") and it lists no change for them, it is asked for that change — given, it goes in in their place; still none, nothing of it goes in beside them, said why, with **Ask again**; what it wrote that says only what your words do not is said so, never *the entry already says that*; a number in words and in digits is one figure; your line held to the same rule; the entry written as your partner wrote it only when your partner had it whole and kept all of it and every break of your paragraphs (an answer giving your entry back in other paragraphs writes nothing) — and the row says what was done — with **Undo these** (the four newest that put something in keep theirs); what **changes your earlier words** (a premise, an entry, a card you wrote, What it's about — your newer word, and it goes in: What it's about, How it reads and Where it's headed are put right in place, with **Undo these**; a premise stays as you typed it, your newer word standing over it); where **the book says otherwise**, with the passage it stands in (a tap goes there) — that waits for your word; what **cannot be, by your world's rules**, with the rule's words; and what is left **for you to decide**. Every quote it gives is looked up as whole words (one shortened with … by its parts): one that stands nowhere, or only in what the helper keeps, is set aside and said row by row, never shown as a contradiction (where it stands only in What it's about or How it reads as the helper keeps them, the helper writes that again from the book); where the look-ups part from how it sorted them, or what it wrote cannot go in, it is asked once more with the rows as they stand — its answer looked up the same way, its entries taking the place of the first answer's by name, the first answer's others going in — and nothing that says what a page says otherwise goes in, however it was sorted (held back under *Not put in*, with **Ask again**), unless, answering the Quick add just before, you said your word stands over that page (the row says which entry it went into, or that nothing did); held only where it speaks of whom the page is about, never where it says what the page says — and your partner's own words, the few sentences it says before anything goes in, show once on the row, its verdict never said again beside them; they lead the row only where they bear it out and every entry went in as written, and otherwise what came of it leads, with your partner's words after it under *Your partner said*; *yours went in* is said only where an entry put in holds your words, and a newer word of yours that went in nowhere says so, with **Ask again**. A name changed everywhere while it answers is carried into what it writes, what it is asked once more and the rows it shows — never a stray entry for the old name, never your entry named back; a quote of the book that still stands as it was stays so. A name that more than one of yours goes by is asked for by its full name, never made a new entry; one found by another name and written as another kind is said so, never written into it (the lore "Order" never into your group "The Order"). Laid again over your entry changed meanwhile in another window, what it adds goes after your words there, never this window's older entry over them. Answer in the same box — *keep mine* is an answer, since what you said before rides with what you say next, in your own voice, with what was held back. While it asks, an update to the app waits, and leaving the page asks first; a row a closed window was asking — in this browser or another — says at once that it stopped. **Ask again** puts your words back as the book names them now, after a name changed everywhere since that row; the row itself still shows them as you typed them. A row from before this keeps the words as you typed them, because the book cannot say which renames came after it. **Clear** lets the conversation go; what went in stays.
- **The notes, the Timeline and the bibliography are one.** Your entry and the notes' card for the same name are one row of the bibliography and open as one card (what the book told under either name), only where they are one — your entry for a group, a place, a thing or lore joins the card of its very name wherever it is filed (a people card "The Order" too), never one whose name another person goes by, and a reading that gives it as a person keeps it among the lore (your entry, where it stands now, what the book told, **Open the card**, **On the Timeline**); a card's own sheet leads to **In the bibliography** and **On the Timeline**. The helper reads each page with your entries for the names in it — your word on what each name means, so a card never says the opposite — unless the page is your own words — most of the words on it now are yours, whether you wrote it or changed it (a word you mend, a name changed everywhere or the writer's words you put back makes no page yours; your words your partner wrote over are your partner's; a few words written again at your note, or a **Go on**, leaves the rest of the words whose they were — words brought in from a file are no words of your hand): then the card follows them — and the notes' check is shown your entries for the cards it checks and the Timeline's newest turns for them: a card that says the opposite of your entry is put right to it (unless a page of your own says otherwise), how people stand agrees with their newest turns, a figure only your entry gives is kept, and a card only your entry names is never taken out. An entry under a card that changes — by your hand, your partner, Quick add, a lorebook or the check itself (a new card for a name your entry already had owes nothing) — has the notes checked against it by itself; notes kept before 2.12.37 are checked against your entries once. Where one of your premises and one of your entries differ, the one you wrote later stands, and the helper and the check are told which — an entry by when each of its sentences was written: a sentence you add or change, or a line you change, is newer, and only those words are said to be — a sentence keeps its time only with the same words in the same order, a, an and the aside: joined with the ones beside it or said again as a few, her name for "she" where your entry calls her "she" or "her", or a slip of the pen put right — a letter put in, left out or two swapped, where the book never writes what it was and does write what it is ("lighthuose" made "lighthouse") — keeps it; the same words turned round, "he" for "she", or any word for another — "loves" made "loved", "is" made "was", "a son" made "sons", "diary" made "dairy" — is newer; when it rides, its kind or a name changed everywhere changes no time; a premise whose time is not known is weighed against nothing.
- **The bibliography** (Notes → **Bibliography**) is what each name in the book means, the way an RPG's codex tells it. ## The checker's bubble

**The judge** holds each passage, in the checker's reading, to ten checks a page of a finished novel should pass — **Opening** (it carries straight on from the passage before), **True to the book**, **Shown** (played as scene, nothing explained that the scene shows), **Pacing**, **Specific**, **Fresh** (no cliché, stock phrase or stock bodily cue, nothing overwritten), **Sentences**, **People** (everyone from their own wants, in their own voice), **Moves** (something has changed by its end), **Ending** (forward, never what it all meant) — and names each one a passage fails with the words that fail it (a tap goes to them). Its grade is the number passed: 9 means one check failed at most — said in the passage's line when you select it (*Passage 3 · graded 9 of 10 · …*), and kept when its fixes go in. A passage's ⋯ → **What the judge said** shows its ten checks — each passed or failed with the words that fail it; a "no" whose words aren't in the passage is shown with what the judge quoted, and not counted. The judge's checks are read by their names in any form, and a quote with a word of its own counts by the longest stretch of six or more of the page's own words in it. A passage under your mark (9 unless you set another, Settings → Who does what) comes behind the bubble with fixes that lift it and **Write it again toward 9**. Where one passage has two takes by two writers for the same moment (you switched the writer and asked for Another take), the judge reads them head to head, blind and both ways round; a win counts only when both readings agree. Folded at the top of Suggestions: each writer's average, its stock wording in every thousand words, its wins and losses head to head, and the checks the book fails most. Nothing is repaired by itself unless you switch that on (**By itself**, below): you decide, in one place. The checker and the judge are told what the writer is told — your words for how it is written (How you like it written, and the book's note at the end: nothing they ask for is ever called a fault) and the chapters before by their summaries, beside the notes and what the book is about. The checker also reads each passage with the story right before it (for a chapter's first passage, the end of the chapter before), and suggests a mend where an opening doesn't carry on.

Why checks and not one number: a judge's whole-page number agrees with expert readers barely better than chance, while checks of craft a reader can point to hold steady (Chakrabarty et al., *Art or Artifice?*, arXiv 2309.14556), and asking which of two pages is better agrees with readers most (LitBench, arXiv 2507.00769). **Stock wording** is counted, not judged — a model reading for it misses most of it (*Measuring AI "Slop"*, arXiv 2509.19163): about 120 families of phrases that stand in machine-made stories hundreds of times more often than in books people write (the Antislop phrase list ranked against EQ-Bench's human baseline, arXiv 2510.15061), and the swivel "It wasn't fear. It was relief." Eleven classic books of fiction (Austen, Chesterton, Melville, Carroll and others — a million words) hold about one in every 80,000 words; machine-made stories one to seven in every thousand.

The round bubble at the bottom right of the page is the **checker**, like Grammarly, made for a book: each passage, once it's finished — the writer's or your own — is read for **Grammar** (spelling, punctuation, the wrong word, agreement, and the book kept consistent: a word spelled two ways, a mark the book doesn't use), **Clarity** (a sentence hard to follow, padded, not natural English, or in a weak passive — written again whole when that is what it needs), wording that sounds machine-made rather than written or flat or out of the book's voice (**Humanize**), your **Words to avoid**, where a passage joins the one before, and slips against the book and its notes (**Continuity**). Grammarly's tone and formality checks are for letters and reports; here the book's own voice is the measure. **Green**: everything is checked and nothing needs changing. **Blue**, its ring turning: it's checking (quickly), or about to (slowly) — it keeps turning with Ambient animation off and with your phone's reduced motion, so a ring standing still never means it is stuck. **Yellow** with a number: suggestions waiting. **Blue** too while something waits its time: a passage its model was too slow to answer for — its checking, its proofreading, a paragraph's speech marks, a chapter read whole, two versions side by side — is asked again by itself once as long as it was waited has passed, said with when ("The checker checks Chapter One, passage 2 again in a minute — its model was too slow to answer for it."), while the rest of its look goes on; one its model will not answer is asked again at the next look; silent for the longest wait twice, or refused twice, it is passed by — a passage set aside in Suggestions with **Check it again**, a chapter or a pair let be until its words change; a model that sent nothing for the longest wait is asked nothing more until as long again has passed, said so, with **Check this chapter** and **Check the whole book** there to ask now. A page written again by itself, or a sentence written again by itself, is held to the same rule: a writer that sends nothing is waited and counted, and let be at the second — never asked again every ten minutes. **Red**: its connection is in trouble (it says why, and tries again by itself). **Grey**: nobody checks (**Settings → Who does what → The checker**).

**The proofreading.** A checking asks for many things at once, and a slip of the hand is the easiest of them to miss (`wassentiment`, `…to ash, She looked at him`). So every checked passage — those checked before this was here too — is read once more for slips alone: a word misspelled, two run together or one split, a space missing or doubled, a word doubled or dropped, two sentences run together with a comma, a capital, a quotation mark. The places that look like slips are named to it. Each fix must be a slip's size — a few letters, marks or words, never a sentence said another way — and goes in like any small fix; one that falls inside a fix found already is mended inside it. The style is never touched: a fragment, dialect, a character's own way of talking, a sound written out, the book's spelling. Each passage's words are proofread once; the words its own fixes make are never proofread again, and the newest chapter is read first.

**The chapter read whole.** A checking reads a passage with as much of the story right before it as its model holds. Once every passage of a chapter is checked, the checker also reads the chapter start to end — with the chapters before by their summaries, the notes and the bibliography — for what disagrees across it: **where people and things are** (who is in the room and who has gone; who sits or stands, how near, on which side; what each holds, wears or carries, and in which hand; a wound, a door, a light, the weather, the hour), **who knows what**, **words said again** (a line remembered or quoted back is the line as it was said), **time**, names and looks, and the seams between passages. It never changes what happens: the later words are put right to the earlier, with the smallest fix — a word, a phrase, a sentence at most. What it finds joins the chapter's suggestions, *Found when the chapter was read whole*. It reads a chapter once it has ended (the next chapter has words, or the writer ended it under Super automatic), when you ask (**Check this chapter**, **Check the whole book**), or once the chapter you are writing has grown by 4,000 words since it was last read whole; again only when its words change — your edits, a rewrite, another take — never for the fixes it put in itself, never sooner than ten minutes after the last time, at most six times a day for a chapter unless you ask. A chapter longer than its model holds is read in parts, each told the chapter up to it — what each part finds is kept as it comes, and a read stopped partway goes on at the part it stopped at; a chapter of one passage is read whole when its checking read it in parts. It is sent the chapter's scene breaks, and told a break marks the jump: a seam it finds right after one waits for you. It reads after the fixes that go in by themselves are in, never on words a fix found that look is about to change. Every ended chapter is read whole by itself, in either mode — writing from before the checker began too. Every fix to the book's continuity — the passage checker's too — quotes the words of the story before it that it holds to: found there (in the passages before it, or its own passage before the words it changes), its card says *As the book says before it: "…"*, and it goes in by itself like any small fix (Apply the checker's fixes by themselves, or Super automatic) — while those words still read so. One that holds only to the notes, quotes words that aren't there, or holds to words changed since, waits for you, and its card says why.

**By itself** (Settings → Who does what, both off until you switch them on): **Apply the checker's fixes by themselves** puts in every fix that has its words as soon as it's found, on the page itself — on every passage checked, from the moment you switch it on — and writes again by itself, on the page too, a sentence with a word on your list or stock wording no fix took away (twice at most for a passage). **⋯ → Put back the words before** takes a fix back out, and words you put back yourself stay as you put them. A slip held only to the notes (one that quotes no words of the book before it — the notes may be what is wrong), a rewrite of a large stretch, more than a small change at once (measured from the words before anything went in by itself), and words brought in from a file wait for you — each card in Suggestions says why. **Write a passage under your mark again by itself** (only for passages written after you switch it on) has the writer write a passage the judge put under your mark again, toward it, with what the judge said it needs — twice at most; then the best-graded version stays on show, every version one arrow away. They never cross: a passage under your mark is written again first and its fixes wait (they were for the words being replaced); a passage that passes, or has had its two tries, gets its fixes. Both reach your own words too, once you switch them on; **Leave it** on a grade stops its lift, a version you put back on show yourself (with the arrows, or by removing the one on show) is never written again by itself, and anything you ask the writer meanwhile goes first — the app's page is taken away whole. What the app writes by itself is written off your page: the words you are reading stay as they are until the new words are whole, then change once; words written again go in whole or not at all.

**Super automatic** (Settings → Who does what, at the top) is one switch for everything that can happen by itself, in every book — you write the premises and read, and play a scene now and then. While it is on: **What it's about** and **How it reads** are kept by the helper (Automatic), the bibliography **fills itself from the book** and **Smart recall** is on, every entry goes **page by page** — Always and When there's room are one: past the bibliography's share, what each page names or needs goes first, and the partner is never asked to set Always (Never, only for you, stays yours; what you set stays underneath for when it is off) — the checker checks even with *Nobody* chosen (the helper's connection does it) — every page of the book, as it does with Super automatic off too, the newest first and a few at a time (the bubble says how many wait), words brought in from a file, as ever, when you ask — **its fixes go in by themselves** on every page it checks. **Your partner's changes land as you set them** (Your instructions → **Your partner's changes land**): at once with Undo, or — set to *When I press Apply* — waiting for Apply, with Super automatic on too. Your own switches stay as you set them underneath — each one shows held by Super automatic — and come back the moment it is off; what was written meanwhile stays. **The chapters by themselves**: the writer is asked, with each page, to end the chapter where the story would — what the chapter is for is done, or a turn a chapter closes on — and says so with a line of its own after the last words, which never reaches the page (a note or the writer's tracking after it is taken off as always; story after it stays on the page, and the chapter goes on). The chapter's end then says *The chapter ends here — your next page goes in Chapter Two*, and your next page — **Write**/**Continue**, **Add as my words**, or a scene played from there and turned into prose — opens the next chapter: made for it, or the one after it while that holds no words yet. **The rest of the chapter** ends it when written to its end; a chapter's opening page, a page between two others, or one cut short never ends one; a rewrite of the page that ended it still ends it, and another take that doesn't lets the chapter go on. Back in a chapter with the one after it written, Write goes on where you are. Your own cards in the notes are kept as they always are: where someone is now and how they stand with others are the helper's, what the book adds rides beside your words as *Since then*, and the notes' check puts your words right where the book contradicts them, quoted — never handed over whole, where a reading could thin a card you wrote or take it out. Never by itself: a passage under your mark written again (its own switch, above), your mark, and your partner's changes when you set them to wait for Apply.

Tap it for **Suggestions**: each one says what it would change and why, with **Apply** or **Leave it**, and **Apply all** at the top. Applying puts the change in on the page itself, with **Undo** — and **⋯ → Put back the words before** on the passage brings back the words before any change, one at a time, with what the checker found in them. What you leave is never offered again for that passage. A word on your list that slipped through — or stock wording no fix took away — is shown in its sentence with **Write it again**, which rewrites just that sentence without it. **Speech marks** are counted on every checked passage — those checked before this was there too, with nothing sent again for them: a paragraph whose quotation marks don't pair is told to the checker; where one mark stands in for the missing quote, the fix is known and needs no model; where only a reading can say where the quote belongs, the checker's connection is asked to add the missing quotation marks and nothing else — its answer is kept only if every letter stays and only quotation marks are added, and then it goes in like any small fix. Its answer may set a mark in the kind its pair needs, or come back in curly marks or with "…" — what goes in is your paragraph's own letters, with only its quotation marks set, in the kind the paragraph uses. One it can't set is offered with **Write it again**, held to the same: what comes back goes in only if nothing but quotation marks changed, and if the writer changed a word, nothing goes in and it says so. A fix of a paragraph's marks you leave is never offered again for that paragraph, and the mender is never asked about it again. The checker is also told how dialogue is set: every spoken line opens and closes, one kind of mark, a new speaker in a new paragraph (it parts a paragraph where a second person speaks), the comma inside the quote before *she said*. **Humanize** looks for what editors fix most in machine-made fiction: awkward phrasing, clumsy or same-shaped sentences, explaining what the scene already shows, cliché, purple lines, vague words where a detail belongs, a closing line that says what it all meant. **Every page is checked by itself, with Super automatic on or off** — what is new first, then writing from before the checker began (when it was first set up, or when you chose a checker after *Nobody*), a few at a time; only words brought in from a file wait to be asked for: **Check this chapter** or **Check the whole book**, checked right after what is new — a page written now never waits behind them. A passage checked while the judge was off is graded by itself once it is on: its grade alone is asked for, and what was found in it stays as it was. **Manual or Super automatic decides what goes in, never whether the book is looked after**: with Super automatic off, what the checker finds waits for you to apply or leave (unless you switch on Apply the checker's fixes by themselves); the notes, the summaries, the Timeline, the bibliography's facts and the page's own repairs keep and heal themselves either way. A passage the fixes went into says so in its line: *the checker's fixes put in*. Versions made only to hold fixes before 2.12.2 went onto the version they were made from, once, when the book first opened. Under the grades, **Why each grade** names each passage's grade and the checks it failed, with the words that fail them; the table counts each writer's own words, and a passage with your words or your partner's in it has a row of its own — a name changed everywhere (one from before 2.12.41 too, marked so once when the book opens — by your partner's conversation where it still keeps the change, else by the words, only where the old name stands on no page and no card — your entries, which a name changed everywhere did not reach before 2.12.41, count for nothing — and the new one is a name of the book: a card's or an entry's, or a word the book writes as a name and never as a word), the writer's words you put back, or a change put back with nothing else of its hand left leaves a page the writer's; any other change of your partner's — a name on one page alone among them — is your partner's.

## The helper's light

The small dot on the **Notes** button is the helper: green when the notes are up to date, amber while it reads, is about to, or waits to ask again what its model was too slow to answer for — a page, a chapter's name, a chapter's summary or its names and figures, a stretch, a summary's check, What it's about and How it reads, the notes' check, what was taken out — saying what waits and when as the time passes, never "up to date" while anything is owed (and, while the whole book is read again, both). Each waits as long as it was waited while the rest goes on; one its model will not answer — a refusal, not a stall — stops only that for the run too, the rest going on, and is asked again in a moment, said so by the light and on its own line ("The helper sums up Chapter One again in a moment — its model would not answer for it the first time."); one its model stays silent on for the longest wait twice, or will not answer twice, is passed by and said so where it shows — a chapter "Not summed up", with **Read it again** under Cards; What it's about with **Write it from the book again**; the notes' check with **Check the notes against the book**. A model that sent nothing for the longest wait (a quarter of an hour) is asked nothing more until as long again has passed — by the helper, the checker or what is written again by itself, on the same connection — and the light and the checker's bubble say so; **Try again now**, **Read it again**, **Check the notes against the book** and **Write it from the book again** ask at once all the same (in Suggestions, **Try again now**, **Check it again**, **Check this chapter** and **Check the whole book**) — red when it couldn't, saying why and how long until it tries again by itself (once that wait is over, "It tries again now.", or, while a page is being written, "It tries again once the page being written is done."), and grey when no helper is chosen. A page its model stays silent on holds nothing else back: the other pages are read, the chapters summed up and the notes checked meanwhile, and it is asked again by itself once as long as it was waited has passed — said with when ("The helper reads Chapter Two, passage 2 again in a moment — its model was too slow to answer for it."). Pages read together that stall are read a passage at a time from then on. Silent twice past the longest wait (a quarter of an hour), or refused, alone, it is set aside: under Cards with **Read it again**, as a page its model will not read is. Once you change its words it is read again, and no longer said to be set aside. While it waits to try again, or has stopped because its connection was turned away, **Try again now** (at the top of the notes) has it try at once (never shown while it only waits its time for a model too slow to answer); the checker's Suggestions have the same. **Undo the last reading** puts back only what that reading changed; a card you edited since stays as you left it. A chapter is named from the page that opens it, however short: the helper names it once a page opens it — written for you, your own words, or a scene turned into prose — and again whenever another page comes to open it (Another take, Retry, Resend, the arrows, Remove versions, the passage taken out by you or your partner, Start a new chapter from here), from that page as it stands: a title of a few words, never your premise copied out. The name a page was given comes back with it, with no call; a scene break taken out changes nothing. A chapter you name, or clear, keeps it — unless Super automatic is on, when the next page to open it names it again. With no helper chosen, its name stays until there is one.

**The book open in two browsers** (the phone and a laptop, say): the helper and the checker work on it in one of them at a time, so nothing is read or checked twice; the other's light says *Another browser open on this book is reading it* (the checker's bubble, that another window or browser is checking it), and the notes and suggestions arrive there when it is done. A page that closes or reloads lets go at once. A page or a reply being written in one browser is never taken in the other for one whose window closed, nor one that stopped kept as still coming, however far apart their clocks are: its heartbeat is kept in the phone's time. A change your partner or Quick add made is said to be made only while the book still reads that way. Where another window changed those words first, the change is laid again where its own words still stand; where they do not, the card says it no longer reads that way. A new entry of a name and kind another window just added is one entry, the new words after the ones already there. Undo of a name changed everywhere puts back a page that was only that rename of newer words, and leaves a page edited again after the rename.

**One connection for everything**: a provider that takes one request at a time refuses your page while the helper or the checker is asking on the same connection — that one now makes way, your page goes once more (said under it), and the helper or checker carries on a little later. A provider that stays silent while its model thinks unseen is given twice the time from then on.

## Connections

Cozy Tavern's connection editor, field for field and in its words. The connection shown says which parts it has (*In use as the writer, the partner, the helper and the checker*), and the list marks those in use; **Who does what** gives the parts. The writer button under the page switches the writer — each connection in its menu says what switching does, and which parts go with the writer. **Settings → Connections → Add a connection**, start from the provider (DeepSeek, Kimi, Synthetic, OpenRouter, Z.ai, Claude, OpenAI, Gemini, Hermes Agent, or anything else that talks like OpenAI), call it something, paste the key, and pick a model from **Fetch what's on offer** or type its name. Every field says under it what it does and what is sent: **Thinking** (Not set — nothing is sent — or Off to Max, each spoken as the nearest level the address takes) and its **Thinking room**; Temperature, Top-p, Top-k, Min-p, the three penalties, **Stop text**, **Seed**; **Longest reply**; **The model's room**; the **prices** for Usage and cost; **Let it look things up** (Claude's own web search or OpenRouter's web plugin — for the pages, the partner and scenes, never the helper; what it looked at is listed under the passage). A number is read the way you type it — "8,000", "200.000" and "128k" for the room, "8/10" or "80%" for your mark; one that is no number is said under its box and never kept as another.

**Start the reply for it (the prefill)**: as written — a started reply, or a thinking seed when it opens with `<think>` — or **Structured**: the model writes your opening itself, held to it by the answer's pattern, so it cannot refuse at the first word or ignore it (addresses that take JSON-schema answers; one that refuses is remembered for its model and gets the prefill as written). Structured has Cozy Tavern's **ready-made templates** (your words open every page; it plans first, unseen; it thinks in your words first; it weighs three paths) and **words it can never write** after your opening. **Keep the thinking open when a seed is sent**, **Workers riding this connection get it too**, the two field names, and **Test it**, which sends a tiny exchange with the prefill exactly as a page sends it and reads what came back. **Test** says whether thinking came back at your level (asking again at the top level when it did not, to tell the level from the address) and times one answer: how soon its first words came and how fast it writes. A model that sends nothing for as long as the helper is waited (three minutes, longer once it has kept the helper waiting) is said to have given no answer in that time — it may only be slow — never "Not working"; a page waits up to sixteen minutes for a word from it, or until you press **Stop**. **Let go** removes a connection and its key, and says which parts it did and who does them after — before you say yes and once it is gone.

**Usage and cost** counts every call — the writer, the helper, the checker, the partner, the narrator — as it lands, by connection and model, today, this month and everything kept, with the money from the prices you set.

What you set is exactly what is sent — temperature, top-p, thinking, the longest reply, prefill and the rest. What you leave empty is not sent at all, so the provider does what it normally does. The only thing ever raised is a floor that stops a page being cut off: a model that thinks gets room to think *and* write, and the helper gets room for its notes.

When a model refuses something (a dial it doesn't take, a request longer than it holds, a different name for the reply's length, thinking at a level it doesn't offer), the request is fitted and sent again, the lesson is kept on the connection, and the page says what was done. A page is never asked again by itself after a real failure: the reason is shown in plain words and you try again when you like.

**Who does what** opens on **Super automatic** (above, under By itself), then chooses the writer (who also narrates scenes), the partner, the helper and the checker; the partner and helper are the writer, and the checker is the helper, unless you choose otherwise (or *Nobody — don't check*). A quick, inexpensive model is perfect for the helper and the checker. Keys stay on the phone: every call goes out through the little server.

**Presets** (top of Your instructions), as Cozy Tavern saves the storyteller's voice: **Save current preset** keeps *How you like it written*, *How the narrator tells scenes*, *How the partner talks*, both notes at the end, *Words to avoid*, your name and your words in the storyteller's own voice together under a name (a Batman, a Light Yagami); **Use** puts every one of them back exactly as saved, with Undo; the one in use is marked; ⋯ saves over, renames, takes it out as a file or removes it (with Undo); **Bring a preset in** reads one back; **The starting words** puts the app's own back in every box. Whether each note at the end is sent, and the modules, stay as they are.

**Your instructions** are sent first, word for word — *How you like it written* to the writer (it starts with who the writer is: a master novelist), *How the narrator tells scenes* to the narrator (emptied, nothing of yours is sent), *How the partner talks* to the partner — like the main prompt of a SillyTavern preset; every box with starting words has **Back to the default** (over words of yours it offers **Copy mine first**, and **Undo** right after puts yours back). **Modules** ride right after them — see below. A note at the end rides after your request, like post-history instructions — **A note at the end — the book** for the writer, **A note at the end — scenes** for the narrator of a scene you play (each empty, or its **Send it** switch off: nothing is sent, and your words wait in the box; the partner gets neither). Each has **Sent as**: a system message (the default, like SillyTavern's post-history instructions), a user message (as if you wrote it after your request), or **the storyteller's own voice** (an assistant message: the page is written on from your words, as SillyTavern sends a note with the role "AI Assistant"). A model that won't take the storyteller's own voice there gets it as a system message — said in What was sent and remembered for that model — and one that won't take a system message there gets it with your request; Claude and DeepSeek with thinking set get it as a system message, since a reply begun in the storyteller's words would skip the thinking. **Words in the storyteller's own voice** (below the notes), as in Cozy Tavern: entries of a few lines the request carries as if the writer — or the narrator, in a scene — had already said them (or as your words, or the setup's), each with its switch, whose words they are, one of three places (after the book and its notes, before the story; right before what you ask; after what you ask, before the note at the end) and who reads them (the writer, the narrator, or both); `{{user}}` and `{{char}}` become the names; off or empty, nothing is sent. **Words to avoid** (one to a line) are told to the writer, the narrator and the partner right after your own words, and the checker marks any that slip through. After your instructions, the book and its notes ride in the system message — or, as you choose (**The book and its notes ride as**), as your own notes in a user message or as the writer's notebook in an assistant message, as in Cozy Tavern.

Everything the app itself says uses your voice and plain words ("our novel", "I'm taking Joon's part"). Your writer, scene narrator and partner can each have their own personality: funny Batman, Hulk, or someone you invented. The app asks them to keep the voice, humour and way of thinking you gave them, while the story’s pages follow the book’s chosen style and viewpoint. Default crafts respect deliberate speech patterns and your version of a familiar character. The model still determines the thoughts it provides.

A connection’s **prefill** is shared by the writer and scene narrator using that connection. Keep identity-specific lines in their separate instruction boxes or **Words in the storyteller’s own voice** when they should be different. Thinking forms in ready-made templates are optional choices; ordinary writing does not add a fixed thinking script.

## The crafts and your modules

**Who is telling**, folded under each of your three boxes (Cozy Tavern's The frame, for the writer, the narrator and your partner each): **Who is telling** — a name, and everything the app says to that teller in your turn greets it by it ("Tony — pick Chapter Two up right where it stops…", "Tony — Bruce here."), and {{char}} in your words is that name; **Grounding phrase** — the first words of its thinking, woven into what it is sent and, where the model continues a started thought (DeepSeek and Kimi with thinking on), planted as the start of the thinking itself, so the thinking you read opens in character — never over a prefill of your own; **The teller thinks as** — I or You, read off your box when it says what the teller is ("I am Tony Stark"): as "I", the app's own rules become the teller's notes to itself, and you are "you" in them; **Say it again at the end** — your box once more just before the note at the end. With a name set, the last look before the page is asked as the teller turning the scene over in its own voice, never as a checklist. **Read my standing words for an assistant's voice** names any line of yours that would make a teller sound like an assistant — nothing is sent, nothing changed. Presets keep all of it.

**Settings → Your instructions → Modules** holds what rides right after your own words. The app's come with it, made from your Simulation Engine preset as Cozy Tavern carries it: **The novel craft** for the writer (your laws turned from a roleplay into a novel — from your premise the writer writes every person, the main character too, keeps the book's point of view, and carries a rewrite's change through), **The scene craft** for the narrator of a scene you play (your character is yours alone), and Cozy Tavern's other rules — **When the scene turns intimate**, **When words won't carry it** (the contest's board, in a scene), **The sound of a voice**, and **Spectacle combat** (off until you choose). Tap one to see its words, who reads it (the writer, the narrator, your partner) and when it rides (always; when the scene turns intimate or a fight starts — it wakes by itself from your own words — what you ask for, and what you asked for or wrote last before that point (a scene break starts afresh), and in a scene where it starts and your last three moves — never from what the writer wrote; or Off). Change its words and they're yours, sent exactly; **Back to the default** (always in the module's sheet, greyed while it is as it came) puts the app's back — **Copy mine first** and **Undo** as for the boxes — and **All back to the default** under the list does it for every one you changed, with Undo — your own modules stay. **Add your own module** for anything of yours.

The crafts are long (about 18,000 tokens each), so a model that holds less than your instructions and modules with room for its answer is never sent the page: it says so in plain words, naming what takes the room and the ways out that would help — lower **Longest reply** when that is what tips it, a model with more room, or a module switched off.

## Keeping it safe

- Every save is numbered; a window holding an older copy can never write over newer work — its change is played again over the newer copy, each step only where the words it changes still read as it found them (your own edit stays your word, the words it replaces kept before it; a name changed everywhere changes the names in your newer words), and a save the phone took whose answer never came is never played twice.
- Every chapter and book keeps its earlier copies (the newest eight, then one an hour for two days, then one a day for a month): chapters list → **⋯ → Earlier copies of this chapter**. Each copy says how it differs from the chapter now ("1 passage more than now"), copies with the same words are one row, the one that reads as the chapter does now is marked, and **Look** shows a copy's passages — a passage not in the chapter now is named so — before **Bring this copy back**. The chapter as it was a moment before is kept as the newest copy.
- A copy of everything is kept once a day (the newest fourteen) and just before each update, each said with its books and words, and anything removed waits in **Settings → Backups → Recently deleted** for thirty days, with its name.
- **Take a copy of everything** downloads one file; **Bring a copy back** adds its books or puts everything back as it was (keeping a copy of what was there first). A book added from a copy keeps its notes as they were read — nothing is read and paid for again.
- What you type while the server is away waits in the browser and is saved the moment it answers — even if Android closed the tab meanwhile.

## Taking the book out

**⋯ → About this book → Take it out as a file**: an e-book (EPUB), a printable book (print it to PDF from the browser), Markdown or plain text. **Bring in a book** on the shelf reads a `.txt` or `.md` file (its chapter headings become chapters — "Chapter 1", "Ch. 1", "Chapter 1, The Letter", "Chapter 1 (The Letter)", "**Chapter 1**", "Part One", a Markdown heading; a line like "Chill." stays a line — and `***` a scene break) or a copy from Backups.
