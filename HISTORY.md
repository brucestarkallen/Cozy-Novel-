## 2.0.0, rebuilt from the ground up

The 1.x studio could not be used day to day, so Cozy Novel was rebuilt as one page and one small server that lives on the phone. The book is chapters of passages; every take, rewrite and edit is a version of its passage, one arrow away. Write, Another take, Rewrite (whole, or just the selected words), Stop and Go on, his own words, scene breaks, writing from the middle and starting a chapter from a passage. A helper keeps the notes (people and where they are now, places, things worth remembering, threads), the chapter summaries and names, with Undo of a reading that puts back only what it changed. A partner to talk the book over with, and Scenes to play one of its people and turn it into prose.

The books live in `~/.cozynovel` on the phone, the same in every browser and every window, live: numbered saves that never write over newer work, earlier copies of every chapter and book, a copy of everything each day, Recently deleted for thirty days, a journal for work typed while the server was away. The model layer is Cozy Tavern's and CozyMaker's: his settings sent exactly, nothing sent that he left empty, thinking per provider, and the lessons a model teaches about itself. The `cozynovel` command updates, replaces an older server on the port, and opens the app; a 1.x book, its notes, drafts, conversations and connections come across by themselves, once, and the 1.x files are only read.

The 1.x documents (SPEC.md, HANDOFF.md, docs/) went with the 1.x code; they remain in the history before this release. AGENTS.md maps the new code. Checked with tests/server.py, tests/app.mjs, tests/thinking.mjs and the two browser walks in tests/browser, on synthetic books with stand-in models; the upgrade was checked against a real 1.x install and its data. No paid provider call and no deployment were made.

Before release day ended, one window came to hold the news line for every window of the browser, so six windows open side by side still save and write at once, and a pull that changed only the page's own files now relights the server by itself; the page it sends names its commit, so every open page knows when newer code is running.

## 2.1.0, his voice, his narrator, a partner that edits

Every word the app sends was rewritten in his own voice ("our novel", "I'm taking Joon's part", "what I want different"), with nothing that names a machine and no second "You are", so a writer he makes Batman or Light Yagami stays itself in its thinking; the book is said once to be fiction for grown readers, written uncensored, and the helper is told the same (Cozy Tavern's frame line, Summaryception's coverage note). Scenes have their own instructions, *How the narrator tells scenes*, with starting words; the scene's frame says whose part he takes, keeps the book's own point of view, and no longer asks to bring in someone already there. Every box with starting words, and the look, has Back to the default. The book and its notes can ride as his notes (user) or the writer's notebook (assistant), as in Cozy Tavern; Claude still keeps the unchanging part ready.

The partner became a co-writer that changes things when asked — a passage (by its number on the page; a new version, his words one arrow away), a chapter's name, what the book is about, a card in the notes — each shown under its answer with Undo, or waiting for Apply as he chooses; it can read a chapter whole that it only had in short. What it's about and How it reads are Manual or Automatic: Automatic keeps, beside his words and never over them, what the book is about and how it reads as it stands, current with every reading; his correction stands until he asks for it again from the book. The notes took Summaryception's best lessons for a novel (first appearances described, feelings that carry, who knows what, causes kept with their effects, how people stand with each other in a new *With others* field), and a card he wrote now stays his through every later reading (it had been his only until the helper's next change to it). A passage written into the middle of a chapter is read as such.

Full screen for the writing page (kept across chapters) and for a scene; the scene's thinking streams open first and folds, as on the page; a scroller at the page's edge shows the passage under the eye and drags through the chapter; each passage's line shows its number and how many tokens its request was, and no longer the connection's name. The models on offer list sits in its own box (it had shown as a bare white control). Checked with all five suites on synthetic books and stand-in models; no paid provider call and no deployment were made.

## 2.1.1, streaming that never fights his hand

Scrolling while a page streams stuttered and jumped: the page pulled itself back down to the newest words every frame until he had scrolled 220 pixels away, so a flick upward was undone before it could take; the whole chapter was drawn again every three seconds for the writer's own saves of what it had streamed, and each time the page was set back to where it stood, which stopped a fling dead; and the passage being written was drawn whole on every frame. The partner and scenes beside the page went to their bottom on every piece of a reply, and were drawn whole every three seconds too.

Now the follow law is Cozy Tavern's (SillyTavern's): the page follows the words only until his hand moves it up, then stays exactly where he put it until he brings it back down or taps Follow the writing, and a finger resting on the page holds it; it follows the passage being written, not the end of the chapter. Words are painted at most once a frame and never more often than four times what the last paint cost, and only the paragraph they land on is drawn again; the thinking streams line by line and follows its own bottom only while he is there. The writer's own saves no longer draw anything again, a passage finishing or a tap choosing another draws only those passages, and the chapter list counts only the passage that changed. Measured on a phone-sized page with the processor slowed four times, streaming 1,500 words into a 25-passage chapter: a flick up stays put (it was pulled back down 104 times), frames over 20 ms fell from 29% to 4%, and the 110–170 ms freeze every three seconds is gone; the partner's answer measured the same way.

Found on the way, and fixed: a record made while the page asked the phone what had changed (when its news line connects, or it comes back from the background) could be taken for deleted when the phone's answer arrived after the record had landed, and every change to it after was dropped without a word — the first connection of a new install was lost this way in 12 of 165 fresh starts in the test runs. An answer that a record is not on the phone now counts only when the page held it as on the phone when it asked and nothing of it was saved since. Checked with tests/app.mjs and both browser walks (three new checks: the follow law, the drawing bit by bit, the record made while asked), on synthetic books with stand-in models; no paid provider call and no deployment were made.

## 2.1.2, Automatic for how much to write

How much to write has Automatic: the writer judges how much the moment needs — all of what he asked for when he said what happens — carries the story forward the way it thinks he'll enjoy it, and stops where he'd most want to take the wheel (a choice that's his to make, a line that waits for an answer, a turn he'd want to steer). Written into the middle of a chapter, it writes as much as it takes to reach what follows. A Claude reply gets a long stretch's room unless he set his own length. The sizes keep asking for a range: a model aims for the middle of a range, where "at most" is read as a target or a reason to rush. Checked with tests/app.mjs (a new check reads the request that went out) and in the browser.


## 2.2.0, a checker like Grammarly, search, words to avoid, and a narrator who takes direction

A checker reads each passage once it is finished — the writer's, his own words, an edit — for grammar, for wording that reads machine-made rather than written (Humanize), for his words to avoid, and for slips against the book and its notes (Continuity). Its bubble sits at the bottom right of the page: green when everything is checked and nothing needs changing, blue while it checks, yellow with the number of suggestions waiting, red when its connection is in trouble, grey when nobody checks. A tap opens Suggestions, each with what it would change and why, Apply or Leave it, and Apply all. Nothing changes until he applies: an applied suggestion is a new version of the passage, the words before one arrow away, and every suggestion of one checking lands on that same version; what he leaves is never offered again for that passage. Suggestions are kept on the chapter for the version they were made for, and only while it still reads the same, so every window and browser shows the same and none lands on words that have changed; a fix whose words do not stand exactly once in the passage, that changes nothing, or that overlaps another is never shown. Writing from before the checker began, or brought in from a file, is not sent unasked: Check this chapter, or Check the whole book (told first how many words that is), does it. The checker is the helper's connection unless he picks another, or Nobody, in Who does what; like the helper it waits out a passing fault and tries again by itself, and a connection refused for good is not asked again until it changes.

Words to avoid, one to a line in Your instructions, are told to the writer, the narrator and the partner right after his own words; one that slips through anyway is marked in its sentence by the checker, with Write it again (that sentence only, rewritten without it). Search, beside Partner, finds any words in the book or in every book — the passages as they stand on the page, chapter names, the notes, About this book, the partner and the scenes — whatever their capitals, accents, quotes or line breaks; a tap goes there, in another book too, and marks the words for a moment. In a scene, Tell the narrator opens a second box for a line about how the telling should go, never his character's words: it rides after his move, marked as his and not the character's, and stays out of the prose. Three choices, a switch beside it, has the narrator offer three different things his character might say or do next, as buttons under its part; a tap plays one, and the offers never go back to the narrator as the story or into the prose.

Checked with all five suites — 14 new checks in tests/app.mjs (what the checker is sent and keeps, older and imported writing, Apply, Leave it and Apply all, a passage changed while it was checked, refusal, retry and Nobody, a passage longer than its model holds read in parts, words to avoid in every request, the line to the narrator, the three choices, search), and new browser walks of the bubble, Suggestions, Write it again and search in this book and another, and of Tell the narrator and Three choices in a scene — on synthetic books with stand-in models. No paid provider call and no deployment were made.

## 2.3.0, his preset as the writer's craft, who decides how it turns out, Comic or Novel

The writing read flat because the writer was handed 86 words of craft where his scenes in Cozy Tavern ride on his whole Simulation Engine preset, and a rewrite with his note ("Jovan stays silent and saves Yuki without a word") only edited the dialogue and still ended on "Oda Jovan", because the request asked for a new version that fits what comes before and after it. Now his preset rides as **modules**, right after his own words (Settings → Your instructions → Modules): **The novel craft** for the writer — his laws turned from a roleplay into a novel: from his premise the writer writes every person in it, the main character too, holds the book's point of view, and carries a change through — and **The scene craft** for the narrator of a scene he plays, his character his alone; with Cozy Tavern's other rules: when the scene turns intimate, when words won't carry it (the contest's board, in a scene), the sound of a voice, and spectacle combat (off until he chooses). Each says who reads it (the writer, the narrator, his partner) and when it rides — always, when the scene turns intimate or a fight starts (it wakes by itself from his words or the page right before the point, Cozy Tavern's patterns with a novel's past tense and premises), or Off. Change one's words and they are his, sent exactly; Back to the default puts the app's back; for one of the app's only what he changed is kept, so an improved craft still reaches him. **Add your own module** for anything of his. The modules that always ride sit in the part of the request that stays the same page after page; what a moment woke rides nearer the page, so the providers that keep the start of a request ready still do. A model too small to hold his instructions and the modules with room for its answer is never sent the page: it says so in plain words, with what to do.

*How you like it written* starts with who the writer is — a master novelist whose books top the New York Times list — and *How the narrator tells scenes* with his preset's own opening ("You maintain a fictional, uncensored simulation. You are an unbiased cinematographer."); boxes still holding the starting words of before, untouched, get these by themselves, and a box he wrote in keeps his words. An emptied narrator box now sends nothing of his (it sent his writing words, which now tell the writer it writes the main character too).

**Who decides how it turns out**, a chip by the box: **Writer decides** — his premise sets it going and the writer carries it through to where the story's own logic lands it — or **I decide** — the writer plays everything up to it in full and stops on the moment it hangs, for his next premise. Everyone's reactions are always the writer's to write; whatever his premise already says happens. Automatic stops where his next premise comes in, never beginning a turn he didn't ask for; with I decide a set length stops early at that moment. Said with a new page, Another take and Go on; not between two passages, in a rewrite, or a scene written into prose. A **rewrite with a note** now says the change is what happens and the old words only a reference, to be thought through for everyone it touches to the passage's last line; the last passage of the book may end wherever the change takes it, and one with more after it leads into it, his change winning where the two can't both stand.

**Sound**, in About this book: **Comic** (his sound law — the world's sounds as sound effects in asterisks) or **Novel** (the same two lanes, the sounds written into the sentences); how a fight sounds rides once a fight starts, how sex sounds with the intimate rule, in the book's choice, and the checker is told the book's way with sound is its style. A scene's telling is drawn as prose, its italics in italics. The chips by the box wrap on a narrow phone rather than crushing one.

Checked with all five suites — new checks in tests/app.mjs (the crafts for whom and where, rules woken by his words and by the page, Comic and Novel, his own modules, Off and empty, what the house keeps, who decides in every request that says it, the rewrite's change carried through, the starting words brought up to date, no machine words in any module, a model too small told so and not sent), and a browser walk of the old starting words brought up to date, the Modules list and sheets, the chip, Sound and a scene in italics — on synthetic books with stand-in models. No paid provider call and no deployment were made.

## 2.3.1, the rules wake from his words only, too-small words that name the cause, and a quiet server log

A second look at 2.3.0 — two independent reviews of the code, and the browser walks run over and over — found these. Each is fixed, and each fix but the two to the walk itself has a check that fails on 2.3.0.

The rules that wake by themselves — when the scene turns intimate, how a fight sounds, the contest's board — were also woken by the page: the last 1,500 characters before the point, the old passage on a rewrite, a scene's whole transcript, the narrator's last turns, read with patterns made for the move he types. Ordinary prose woke them: "shot him a look", "it struck him that", "a stab of guilt", "a heart attack", "fighting a smile", "a fight about money" woke the fight; "cocks his head", "mounts her horse", "summa cum laude", "Dick laughed" woke the intimate rule — so on a quiet page the writer could be told how bodies are written during sex. Now only his own words wake them, as in Cozy Tavern: what he asks for, with the premise he is carrying on or a passage of his right before the point (a scene break or the chapter's start begins afresh); a rewrite, his note with the words of his that made the passage; in a scene, where he said it starts (for the whole scene) and his last three moves and lines to the narrator; a scene written into prose, where he said it starts and his moves in it (kept beside the passage for another take). Plain English that only sounds like a fight or sex is set aside first — only the whole idiom, so "fights the fear demon", "stabs at his throat", "mount an attack" and "undresses him" still wake — and "Jovan and Kenjaku fight", "they duel", "keeps fighting" and "fights to the death" now wake the fight, while "they fought about money", "her parents fought" and "they had a fight" do not. With the book riding as his notes or the writer's notebook, a rule waking or going back to sleep changes the system message before the book, so that one request is not kept ready by the providers — the next one is; 2.3.0 said they always were.

A model too small: the words named only his instructions with the modules, even when the room for the answer (his own Longest reply, or a thinking model's room to think), a long talk or the chapters the partner asked to read took the room; told a partner that reads no module to switch one off; and sent the narrator to a row Who does what does not have. Now they name what takes the room, the numbers adding up, and only the ways out that would help — lower Longest reply first when that is what tips it; a model with more room for the writer (who tells his scenes) or the partner, by its row's name; a module switched off only when one rides for it; a shorter message, or Start over. Chapters the partner asks to read that are more than its model holds beside everything else — his words, the book's words and notes, the chapter open, the talk, the answer's room — are sent cut to fit: the short ones whole, the long ones sharing what is left, each keeping its opening and its end with the middle said to be left out; 2.3.0 sent nothing.

"Too long" from a model that names a size no smaller than the room the request was made for (it counts the words higher than this app does) refused the page with a number of the app's own guessing — "holds about 24,576 tokens" for a 32,768 model — and did the same on every page after. Now the book is fitted smaller, never below what has to go with it, and sent; that guess is kept for fitting the book only, never as a reason to refuse a later page. A model that names a size smaller than the room is still refused with its own number when even what has to go cannot fit in it.

Another take on a passage with only a scene break after it, or a page written after it, was asked as a bridge "between the text before it and the text that follows it" and lost who decides how it turns out: a scene break or an empty page is not text to bridge to. A narrator box he emptied before 2.3 (spaces count as empty, as 2.2 read it) meant "the narrator reads How you like it written", and 2.3.0 sent nothing of his instead: once, such a box gets his own writing words (never starting words), and a box he empties from now on stays empty.

The server wrote a traceback into its log (~/.cozynovel/server.log, the lines the launcher shows when the server will not start) every time a page went away before its answer — a tab closed mid-save, a kept-alive line the browser let go. Nothing was lost (a save lands whole or not at all, and the page asks again), but the log filled with noise that could bury the real reason; a page leaving is no longer written down, and any other fault still is. A check in the main browser walk failed once in about thirty runs on a 409 that Chromium logs as a console error: 409 is the server's designed answer to a save from an older copy (the page plays its steps over the newer copy and saves again), and two windows, or a page and its own reload, saving one record close together can always cross. The walk sets those answers from the store aside — three at most — and still fails on any other console error. Another of its steps, seen failing once in about forty runs (it could since 2.2.0), expected the page to meet a model's refusal of Top-k first; but the checker rides the same connection and reads a rewritten passage a few seconds after it, so now and then it met the refusal first, and the page — rightly, since what one of them learns about a model holds for all of them — went without Top-k from the start. The walk now counts every request to that model and checks what matters: his Top-k was sent, it was left out once refused, the page came, and the next page leaves it out from the start (shown to fail when the lesson is not kept).

Checked with all five suites — new checks in tests/app.mjs (prose never wakes a rule and his words do, in every request that wakes one; a list of premises and plain English; the too-small words for each cause; "too long" at the model's own size, and the next, longer page sent; the scene break; the narrator box; the partner's reading fitted beside the book's words and notes) and tests/server.py (forty pages leaving mid-save) — on synthetic books with stand-in models. No paid provider call and no deployment were made.

## 2.4.0, each version its own notes, Cozy Tavern's connection editor, presets, and the partner as its housekeeper

Asked: the notes kept the old version's facts after a rewrite — another take was even written with them — and lit some cards for no clear reason. The cause sat in three places. The helper read a new take of a passage it had read as an edit of the old one, so what the old take made up (a name said aloud, who knows what) stayed in the notes; the writer of the new take was handed those notes; and nothing remembered what each version had put there, so the arrows could not bring a version's notes back. Now **each version of a passage keeps its own notes**, like Cozy Tavern's ledger checkpoint for every swipe (`memory.readings[passage][version]`: each card the reading touched, before and after, and what it kept of the book). Another take, a Rewrite or his Edit of the newest passage takes the old version's reading back out first — card by card, only where the card still reads as that reading left it — and the new version is read from there, as new; the writer of the new take is handed the notes as they stood before the passage. The arrows back to a version read before, and taking out the newest passage, settle the notes at once with no reading; Undo of a passage taken out brings its notes back the same way. A passage further back is read as a change, told what it used to say, so later readings are never written over; a card he wrote or changed stays his through all of it. The book's newest passage is read on its own, so its version's notes are its own. A chapter that reads again as it once did gets its summary from then back, with no call. **Readings — each version's notes**, at the foot of the notes, lists every reading newest first, with what it changed and whether it is in the notes now; **Put back** takes out only what one reading changed, **Read again** has the helper read that passage afresh. Lit cards meant "changed by the last reading", never "sent": the notes now say so — every card goes to the writer, lit or not — and mark each lit card *new* or *updated*, and his own *yours*. When the notes have not read the newest passages yet, the writer is told which ("Not in these notes yet", Cozy Tavern's), and a page about to be written waits up to five seconds for a reading already on its way.

**Cozy Tavern's connection editor**, field for field and in its words: thinking (Not set sends nothing) and its room, every dial, stop text, seed, longest reply, the model's room, prices, **Let it look things up** (Claude's web search or OpenRouter's web plugin, for the pages, the partner and scenes, never the workers; what a page looked at is listed under it; how many look-ups is sent only when he sets it), and the prefill — as written, or **Structured** (the opening held by the answer's pattern, read as it streams; a model that refuses it is remembered), with the ready-made templates made for a novel, words it can never write, the two field names, keep the thinking open, workers get it too — with its own **Test it**. A template that goes as written (an address that takes no pattern, a model that refused one, a worker) sends only its plain opening, never its markers (Cozy Tavern M582/M586). **Test** asks again at the top level when no thinking came back, and times the first words and the writing speed. **Usage and cost** counts every call by connection and model, today, this month and in all. Every button and menu item says in a small line what it does.

**Presets**: Save current preset keeps How you like it written, How the narrator tells scenes, How the partner talks, both notes at the end, Words to avoid and his name together; Use puts them back exactly, with Undo; the one in use is marked as he types; saved over, renamed, taken out as a file, brought in, or removed with Undo; The starting words puts the app's own back. Each note at the end has a **Send it** switch: off, his words wait in the box and nothing is sent. Every module's sheet always has **Back to the default** (greyed while it is as it came), and **All back to the default** under the list puts back every one he changed, with Undo.

**The partner as Cozy Tavern's housekeeper, for a book.** Besides a passage's words or all of it, it can take a passage out, change the same words across chapters (whole words only), rename someone everywhere at once (never an ordinary word, never inside the name it becomes or a hyphened name), write a chapter's summary, rename a card (only one that is there), change a module's words when asked; and read a chapter whole, every place some words stand, or a module, before it answers — in any round of an answer. A change that missed its words is told back to it in the same answer (twice at most, every answer's changes made); an answer that says it changed the book with no changes in it is asked once for the whole answer again, which stands in its place (quoted words and talk such as "I've added four directions below" are not claims). A round that fails keeps the answer in hand and its changes. Set to wait: **Edit by hand**, **Apply all**, **Leave all**; **Undo all** for an answer's changes, newest first; an Undo that can put back only part says what stayed; **What it said, whole**. **Conversations**, as Cozy Tavern keeps sessions: the open one by name, the others newest used first, **New**, **Rename**, **Copy**, **Remove** with Undo, **Branch from here** under an answer (a copied change stays the conversation's it was made in), **Edit and ask again** under his message, with Undo; **Start over** empties the open one, with Undo, the changes it made staying in the book. Search finds words in every conversation and opens the one they are in; the partner can go full screen. **Quick asks**: check the notes against the book, tidy the notes, find what contradicts itself, four directions, a character's psychology, critique this chapter, what the next chapter should do.

Found on the way, and fixed: a word typed into a Settings box and then, within half a second, a preset used or the sheet drawn again, was written in afterwards over what had just been put there (every typed field in Settings now lands its words first); the presets row did not change when the boxes did; the readings of a chapter not open in the window, or of a passage taken out while another chapter was being read, could be thrown away as "not in the book"; Undo of a passage taken out left its words kept as taken out, so taking it out again later could tell the helper what it said the first time. ## 2.4.1, the notes' auditor, the judge, seams that hold, a partner that knows its part, Usage and cost as Cozy Tavern shows it

Asked: a book still said Jovan Oda said his name aloud. The cause: its notes were kept before 2.4.0, when no reading remembered which version a fact came from, so the per-version notes had nothing to take back — the old take's facts stayed. Now the notes have **an auditor**, as Cozy Tavern audits its ledger: every six readings, whenever he asks (**Check the notes against the book**, at the top of the notes), and once by itself for notes kept before it — the first time such a book opens — the helper holds the whole notes against the book as it stands, the versions on the page, and puts right what no passage says any more or has drifted (who said what, who knows what, where someone is now, threads closed or missed). His own cards keep his words and are never taken out; what it changed is lit, with Undo the last reading.

The goal is a finished novel, so the seams have to hold. The writer is told its first lines follow straight on from the last ones — the same moment, everyone where it was left, nothing told twice; a scene turned into prose is told the same, and kept in the book's own point of view and tense whoever he played, changing whose eyes we're behind only at a scene break. The checker (the bubble) now reads each passage with as much of the story right before it as its model holds, holds the book's own words over notes that lag, and suggests a mend for an opening that doesn't carry on from the passage before it.

**The judge**, Cozy Tavern's benchmark for a novel, on unless he turns it off (Settings → Who does what): in the checker's own reading, each passage is graded 0–10 for prose, people, continuity, pacing and pull, and as a whole. A passage under his mark (9 unless he sets another) comes behind the bubble with fixes of its own (Craft) and **Write it again toward 9**, which writes it again with what the judge says it needs — the words before one arrow away; or Leave it. Nothing is repaired by itself: one place, the bubble, where he decides. The grades, folded at the top of Suggestions, show the average and which writer writes best.

The partner is told its part plainly: it knows the book, says what it thinks, keeps the book straight by changing it when asked, and leaves the next pages to the writer. A premise it offers for the next page comes in a box of its own with **Use as the next direction**, which puts just that premise under the page; an answer with none has no such button. Under each answer only **Another answer** shows, with **⋯** for Branch from here, What it said whole (when it made changes), What was sent, Copy and Remove; under his message, **Edit and ask again** and **⋯**.

**Usage and cost** as Cozy Tavern shows it: Today, Last 7 days or Last 30 days, one at a time, as a small table (connection · model, in, out, cost, and the total); what it comes to per day, week and month at the rate so far; the busiest day per model, folded until he opens it.
