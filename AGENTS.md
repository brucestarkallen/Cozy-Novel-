# Working on Cozy Novel

# ⚠️ THE OWNER'S TIME COMES FIRST — NO HOURS OF TESTING ⚠️

## A FIX THAT TAKES HOURS IS A FAILED FIX. HE IS WAITING, AND THE WAIT HURTS HIM.

**An update takes MINUTES, not hours.** He has waited five hours for one fix
while every suite in the repo ran over and over, and then the fix still did not
work. Making sure there is no bug or regression matters — but a long run of
tests that the change cannot reach proves nothing and costs him everything.

- **FIND THE ROOT CAUSE FIRST, BY READING THE CODE.** Tests do not find the cause; reading does.
- **RUN ONLY THE TESTS THE CHANGE REACHES.** A new test for the change, the tests named for that feature (`ONLY=words node tests/app.mjs`), and the one or two suites for that part of the app. **NEVER the whole repo for a small fix.** Never every browser walk.
- **NEVER RUN THE SAME SUITE TWICE "TO BE SURE".** Once, on the final code.
- **A TEST THAT FAILS ON THE CODE ALREADY PUSHED IS NOT YOURS** — say so in one line and ship.
- **SHIP, THEN SAY WHAT YOU DID NOT RUN.** He would rather know what was skipped than wait for it.
- **ANSWER HIS QUESTION FIRST, THEN WORK.** Never go silent for an hour.

Read this before changing anything. It is the map of the code and the rules the
owner keeps. The owner reads plain words, not jargon: name things the way the
screen names them.

## What it is

One page (`index.html`, `css/novel.css`, `js/`) served by one Python file
(`serve.py`) that also keeps the data. No build step, no packages, no
framework. It runs in Termux on the owner's phone; the browser is only a
window onto the server.

## The rules (the owner's, not negotiable)

- **The goal is a finished novel.** Continuity has to be perfect: the notes true to the book as it stands, the summaries good, and every passage carrying on smoothly from the end of the one before it. The writer is told to follow straight on from the last words; the checker suggests a mend wherever a seam doesn't hold; the notes' auditor puts the notes right.

- **Everything he set is sent exactly; what he left empty is not sent.** The only thing ever raised is a floor that prevents a cut-off page (thinking room, the helper's room).
- **His own instructions come first, word for word, in the system message** — then his words to avoid, when he listed any, then the modules (Settings → Your instructions → Modules: the novel craft for the writer, the scene craft for the narrator, his own). The book and its notes ride after them — in the system message, or as he chooses as his notes (user) or the writer's notebook (assistant). A rule that wakes (an intimate scene, a fight) rides in the system message's moving part, never as his words.
- **Every word the app says is natural and in his voice** — never "AI", "assistant", "user", "system", "prompt", "persona", "role", "the author", never a second "You are": his writer may be Batman. Who the writer is (a master novelist) is said only in the starting words of his own box, his to change. The crafts and rules are held to the same words. The book is said once to be fiction for grown readers, uncensored; the helper is told the same, so it never softens what it keeps.
- **Every setting with starting words has Back to the default.**
- **Every button and menu item says in a small line what it does** (Cozy Tavern's quiet lines): a menu item's `hint`, a settings button's quiet line under it.
- **The judge is held to evidence.** It answers checks a reader can verify, names the words that fail each, never grades by length or ornament, reads two takes head to head both ways round, and says so when it is judging its own model's writing. What can be counted (stock wording) is counted, never left to its reading.
- **The notes heal themselves.** Every few readings, and once for notes from before the auditor, the helper holds the whole notes against the book as it stands and puts right what no passage says any more.
- **Each version of a passage keeps its own notes.** The writer of another take or a rewrite is handed the notes as they stood before that passage; a version written again, picked or taken out is settled from the readings kept wherever that is exact (the newest read passage), and read as a change otherwise. A card he wrote or changed is never written over by any of it.
- **A page is never asked again by itself after a real failure.** Lessons (a refused dial, "too long", a renamed length field) fit the request and send it again; so does a page refused as busy while the helper or the checker was asking on the same connection — the app's own doing: the worker gives way and the page goes once more; anything else says the reason in plain words. Only the helper and the checker wait out a passing fault and try again by themselves; a connection refused for good is not asked again until it changes.
- **Nothing he wrote is ever lost:** versions, never overwrites; the edit box keeps its words on Back; an empty answer leaves no empty passage; earlier copies and Recently deleted for everything. The checker only suggests: nothing changes until he applies it — or until he switches on Apply the checker's fixes by themselves / Write a passage under your mark again by itself (Who does what), or Super automatic: the fixes go in on the page itself on every passage whose fixes wait while it is on, whenever it was checked (what waited in Suggestions from before the switch goes in at the next look), written before too, the words before kept on the version (⋯ → Put back the words before); a passage under his mark is written again only when written since the switch — his own words too (his word, 2.6.2) — and what he leaves stays left.
- **The data lives on the device, the same in every browser.** Browser storage holds only per-browser conveniences (which columns are docked, which sections are folded) and the journal of work not yet on the phone, which goes to the phone the moment it answers.
- **Updates land without a manual refresh**, and the app repairs what it can detect.
- **Manual or Super automatic decides what goes in — never whether the book is looked after** (his word, 19 October 2026: "manual is for someone who prefers to decide what to apply and not; Super automatic is for me, who doesn't want to think about anything — but manual doesn't mean something is not automatic and self-healing"). With Super automatic off, he decides: the checker's fixes, where a chapter ends, What it's about written by him, an entry's Always. His partner's changes follow his own setting (Your partner's changes land) in both modes — Super automatic never lands one he set to wait (his word, 19 October 2026, 2.12.39). Everything else runs by itself in both: the checker looks at every page and grades it, the chapter is read whole, the proofreading, the notes and their check, the summaries, the Timeline, the bibliography's facts (while Fill it from the book by itself is on), the page's own repairs. A new switch or gate on `superOn` is for a decision only.
- **One button, one meaning; controls named the way the screen names them;** busy sections fold closed; no debug surfaces; no content filters.
- **The phone's Back closes what is open, top first, before it leaves the book.** Every overlay goes through `onBack()` in `js/ui/dom.js`.
- **A room is one still picture, and what moves comes after everything still.** A moving piece makes the phone keep a screen-sized picture of everything drawn after it; the desk's veil lies under what moves, only the shelf's over it; nothing is ever laid over a room's moving pieces on the desk. The document never scrolls (the shelf and the page scroll in their own boxes), so a phone browser's bar never moves the room. `tests/browser/walk_performance_worlds.py` holds the desk to 90 MB of such pictures at a phone's density.
- **Nothing heavy on the page's thread for the same words twice.** The page draws on one thread; the helper and the checker work on it too. A whole book folded, counted or fingerprinted is kept by its text (`names.js foldLong`, `util.js counted`), and a panel that cannot be seen is not drawn (`panes.js leftVisible`, `sideVisible`). Measure with a book of 300,000 words before saying a pass is cheap (`tests/browser/profile_performance.py`, or a CPU profile at 4×).
- **Streaming never fights his hand.** While words stream, the page (and the partner and scenes beside it) follows them only until he moves it up; from then on it stays where he put it until he brings it back down or taps Follow the writing. A stream repaints only the paragraph its words land on, and nothing is drawn again for the writer's own saves of what it has streamed.
